# Guides

Three guides in English explain how to count telework days for someone who lives in France and works in Geneva or Luxembourg, and how employers can keep a day-by-day record. They use the thresholds already on this site and the official sources listed on each guide.

- [Living in France and working in Geneva: how to count telework and mission days](https://borderday.com/en/guides/france-geneva-telework-days/)
  Short answer: If you live in France and work for an employer in Geneva, telework from France stays taxable in Switzerland up to 40% of your annual working time in a calendar year. That 40% can include up to 10 days of temporary missions in France or another country. Above 40%, the pay for your telework days becomes taxable in France from the first telework day, not just the excess.

- [Living in France and working in Luxembourg: how to count telework days](https://borderday.com/en/guides/france-luxembourg-telework-days/)
  Short answer: If you are tax resident in France and employed in Luxembourg, you can spend up to 34 days per tax year physically working in France or a third country and your whole salary stays taxable in Luxembourg. Any part of a day counts as a full day, and part-time or part-year work reduces the 34 in proportion. Above 34 days, France can tax the pay for the time you actually worked outside Luxembourg, from the first day.

- [How to track cross-border telework days](https://borderday.com/en/guides/how-to-track-cross-border-telework-days/)
  Short answer: Employers of cross-border staff need a day-by-day record of where and how each employee worked, because tax and social security rules each use their own count. In Switzerland, employers of France residents (outside the 1983 cross-border agreement) must track telework and mission days actually worked and report an annual rate, even when it is 0%. In Luxembourg, every telework arrangement of a non-resident employee must be declared to social security.
